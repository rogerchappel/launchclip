# Roadmap

LaunchClip's goal: **a SaaS founder points it at their product and gets a launch-ready video, first time.** This roadmap is ordered by what gets founders there fastest. Dates aren't promised; items move as we learn.

## Shipping now: 0.3.0

- One prompt (plus your assets) to a finished video, through your AI agent's subscription or headless (`launchclip make`)
- No API keys needed: the voice, music, sound effects and rendering are built in and local; ElevenLabs, OpenAI and Anthropic are optional upgrades
- It picks the kind of video from your inputs: a motion-graphics explainer, a product demo, or an edit over your footage
- Style packs, five starter styles, and styles drafted from your previous videos
- A world camera, motion grammars, ambient motion and 14 caption styles
- Raw-footage base edit: silences, bad takes and filler words removed, audio cleaned up
- Self-checking and self-repair before you see the video
- Change it by chatting; only the changed scenes re-render
- Export to Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or OpenTimelineIO
- **Demo autopilot:** point it at your SaaS codebase and it starts the app, records the key flows on safe demo data, and edits them in with zooms and callouts timed to every action ([guide](guide/demo-autopilot.md))
- **Subject-aware effects:** in a talking-head recording, graphics can sit behind you (your product name behind your head, your app on the wall behind you, a dolly zoom), chosen by the art director like any other module, or put on a clip yourself with `launchclip fx` ([guide](guide/effects.md))
- **Motion that is checked:** a motion check fails a plan on easing, stagger and exit faults before the render, and every motion grammar sets travel, exit ratio, follow-through, parallax and hero dim
- **Exploded-view scenes**, nineteen backdrop plates, hand-drawn marks on a title and a light leak over a cut
- Research finds openly licensed images on Openverse, with no key
- Four npm packages, versioned together: `launchclip`, `@launchclip/core`, `@launchclip/remotion` and `@launchclip/engine`

## Next

- **Approval packs and review in Slack** (built; in the next release; [guide](guide/approval.md), [RFC](APPROVAL_RFC.md)): gather a batch of finished carousels, images and videos into one folder with every platform's files and captions, post each one to a Slack channel, and read the reviewers' reactions and replies back as decisions. A CLI with no server, so any scheduler can run it nightly.
- **Carousels and single-image posts** (built; in the next release; [guide](guide/carousels.md), [RFC](CAROUSELS_RFC.md)): `launchclip carousel make "<brief>"` plans, renders, checks and exports a swipeable carousel or one designed image with its post caption and alt text, for Instagram, TikTok photo mode, LinkedIn (PDF or images), Facebook, X, Threads and Meta carousel ads. Templates follow proven feed styles (plain notes, cinematic photo pages, numbered steps, editorial paper, quotes and more) on your brand, every slide is checked (a cover that makes one promise, type large enough for the feed, contrast on the rendered pixels, safe margins, copy without filler), and video carousels are separate short clips per card, from your footage, a drawn animation or a generated clip. Slides can sit on generated photos or backgrounds under a brand-coloured gradient, and elements or one wide image can run across slides. Still to come: a measured first-pass rate for the planner, and carousels learned from how posts perform.
- **A fresh look for every project** ([RFC](SEEDED_STYLES_RFC.md)): with no style or brand, each project gets its own generated style (palette, fonts, backgrounds, motion, captions, characters), saved so it stays the same; reroll it, pin a seed, or keep it as your own style.
- **Videos that look designed, not decorated** ([RFC](VIDEO_QUALITY_RFC.md)):
  - Pacing and density are measured from strong reference videos, per format: Shorts, long form, your own footage and motion graphics.
  - Every line of the story gets a visual decision, and footage gets split-screen layouts and a much larger visual vocabulary.
  - Styles carry your brand frame and a few simple dials, and a draft that's sparse or off-style fails its checks before you see it.
  - Fully animated stories get the same treatment once the animated characters below arrive: a cast directed line by line, inside the same checks.
- **Animation from one prompt** ([RFC](ANIMATION_PIPELINE_RFC.md)): describe a story and get a character animation back, a Short first and longer pieces later.
  - A writers' room turns the prompt into a script with dialogue, and a show bible fixes the cast, world and style before any shot is planned.
  - Every shot is drawn in code, so a character looks the same in every shot and the acting is timed to the voice. You can approve the cast and a rough cut before the final render.
  - Made your own backgrounds, character art or finished shots somewhere else? Hand them in. They are checked against each character's reference sheet, and anything missing or off falls back to the code-drawn version.
  - It works through your AI agent's subscription or headless: the model writes, designs the cast, directs and reviews.
- **Viral formats, results built in** ([RFC](VIRAL_FORMATS_RFC.md)): proven short-form structures ("$1 a second", "the last time", "X vs Y") as first-class formats, each with its hook, beats, key shot, share moment, voice tier and packaging. The planner picks a format and a fresh angle before the concepts, offers only formats the kit can actually render, and the finish gates check the first frame, the key shot and the share beat, all at no extra cost.
  - Still to come, for character story Shorts:
    - A length target of about 20–30 s and 40–70 spoken words. The short-video rules cover anything up to 90 s, but nothing pulls a story short down to that length.
    - A "snap" beat after three escalations, where the pattern breaks and the obvious ending flips, before the loop back to the opener.
    - A works-on-mute check: the setting and the problem read from the pictures and on-screen text alone, asked as a critic question on the storyboard.
    - A "When [character] [does the thing]" template among the five title options when a video has a recurring character.
- **Smarter asset understanding:** recognise what's in your screenshots and recordings (a pricing page, a dashboard, an onboarding flow) and place them where the story needs them.
- **Built-in critic by default:** a vision model reviews the video the way a human editor would, as part of `launchclip make`.
- **Faster first run:** smaller downloads and pre-warmed rendering, so the first video arrives sooner.
- **Windows support:** tested and supported alongside macOS and Linux.

## Planned: richer, faster, louder videos

Five R&D documents, each built in small steps that work on their own. Every visual here is drawn the same by both renderers.

- **Sparks, hits and pixel effects** ([RFC](VFX_LAYER_RFC.md)):
  - Sparks, bursts, smoke and glow that land on the beat, on any scene, each with a matching sound on the same frame.
  - Celebration moments for the payoff: combo, critical hit, level up, achievement.
  - Nine new transitions: burn away, slash, shatter, CRT off, glitch, blinds, diamonds, star iris and pixel dissolve.
  - A retro pixel look and style for game-flavoured videos.
  - Weather backgrounds: rain, snow, fog, fireflies, aurora and god rays.
  - Effects on a character or a card: freeze it, electrify it, scan it, dissolve it.
  - More looks for the same moments, beside the pixel one: Lottie animations (vector sparkles, bursts, hand-drawn pops and confetti, recoloured to the brand) through the Lottie players both renderers already have, and a soft, painterly look that draws the same effects with CC0 particle textures (smoke, flame, sparks, stars). Each look keeps the same timing, peaks and sounds, and every Lottie file's licence is recorded.
- **Taste checks** ([RFC](VIDEO_TELLS_RFC.md)):
  - The registry of video tells below, as data: from purple gradients and glowing cards to a fade on every cut and a title card for a hook, each checked on the plan or the render, each with its fix. Our own defaults are fixed first: the cyan-on-near-black brand, bouncy eases in the module catalogue, the 27% overshoot in the `kinetic` feel, the big-number-small-label stat card and the "Chapter N" kicker.
  - A copy check for tired phrasing: the script and every on-screen line are checked for buzzwords, stacked "Not this. That." lines and dash-heavy sentences, and the planner's own examples stop teaching them.
  - An art direction before the first scene: what the subject is, two or three words of character, its world, the one or two rich moments and one signature detail, with the hero of every scene marked.
  - Three opening stills that differ in structure, one choice, then the full render.
  - A stricter critic: it judges the video before it sees the automatic findings, can ask for new evidence instead of a repair, and after a repair only scores whether each problem was fixed.
  - A less common default body font.
- **Sharper plans and a faster hook** ([RFC](PLAN_AND_PACE_RFC.md)):
  - Plans say the idea in one line, name the one object that carries the film from scene to scene, and list what this film must never do; each is checked.
  - Exact words and worked examples are locked once and checked in every scene, and every transition says what it shows.
  - Hooks are measured: the opening seconds are busier than the rest, no hold runs long, and moves land on the beat, not a frame after it.
  - Planned silences, early sound and one held breath, checked in the mix.
  - Reference videos are read for their real transitions, entrance timing, sound effects and tempo, and our renders are checked against the same measures.
  - Cuts land on calm moments near a beat.
- **Immersive sound** ([RFC](IMMERSIVE_SOUND_RFC.md)):
  - Real recorded instruments for the gentler genres, downloaded only when you ask, with the credits written for you.
  - Graphics that move with the music: bars, glows and pulses driven by the same measured sound in both renderers.
  - Charts you can hear: every bar and point plays a note in the video's key.
  - Directed sound: silences before big moments, the next scene's sound arriving early, transitions made of sound, and every sound paired with something you see.
  - A cleaner master: an oversampled peak limiter, and safe handling of silent or very loud mixes.
  - Optional sound effects generated on your own machine, with their licence shown.
- **A finishing pass, new looks and new elements** ([RFC](FINISHING_AND_ELEMENTS_RFC.md)):
  - A finishing pass for every video: bloom, lens fringing, zoom blur, film burn, a CRT screen, halftone and grain, applied the same way whichever renderer drew it, with captions kept sharp.
  - Colour grades and `.cube` LUTs at the end of the render, a grade to suit each style.
  - More backgrounds, idle motions and transitions: fog, caustics, cells, bokeh, clock wipes, card flips, pendulums and pulses.
  - New looks: risograph, one-bit dither, pen plotter, LiDAR scan and stained glass.
  - Maps that travel: a glowing route that lights each country as it arrives, with the camera following.
  - New elements: words that build structures, a machine that works through a proof, a persistent progress bar, scrambling text, one shape that morphs through the whole video, flow-field backdrops and your own Lottie files.
  - Optional local image and video generation for plates and drafts, on your own machine and limited to openly licensed models.
  - Skills that trigger reliably, checked with test requests (and the Remotion skill's description cut to fit the 1,024-character limit), and a critic that compares two renders blind.

## Later

- **More languages:** voices, captions and on-screen text in multiple languages from one storyboard.
- **Avatar presenters:** a generated presenter for founders who'd rather not be on camera (building on the existing HeyGen avatar support).
- **Animated characters:** a built-in cast for explainers and story-led promos ("someone hits the problem, then your product fixes it"), drawn in code so a character looks the same in every shot. One rig, many people: each character is a mix of build, skin tone, hair, outfit and accessories, with a few dozen ready-made, coloured by your style pack and checked on a cast sheet. The planner directs them with named poses and expressions cued to the narration, in a handful of drawing styles and in sets that fit a product story (a desk and laptop, a phone, a screen showing your own app). It works with both renderers.
- **Collections and batches** ([RFC](COLLECTIONS_RFC.md)): group videos by product, channel, client or campaign, with one style, voice and set of rules shared by all of them; save a video you like as a recipe and make more like it in batches; approve the scripts in bulk before anything renders; save projects to any drive; and a machine-wide queue that runs only as many renders at once as your hardware can take, from the CLI or an agent.
- **Launch kits:** one run produces the launch video plus cut-downs for every platform (YouTube, Shorts, Reels, TikTok, X, LinkedIn, Product Hunt), thumbnails and captions files.
- **Brand from your website:** point it at your landing page and it builds your style pack from your real colours, fonts and tone.
- **Scheduled updates:** re-record the demo and refresh the video automatically when your product changes.
- **Partial re-rendering for HyperFrames** (Remotion already has it). The installed HyperFrames 0.7.111 has no frame range; 0.9 adds `--start` and `--end`. See DUAL_RENDERER_PLAN.md, "Open renderer gaps".
- **Videos that follow a zoom or an iris in HyperFrames.** A single-video module can stay in its scene layer and follow every transition; measured in DUAL_RENDERER_PLAN.md, "Open renderer gaps".

## Ideas we're exploring

- A cross-model benchmark: how well do different AI models plan videos with LaunchClip?
- A hosted version, for founders who don't use an AI coding agent at all.
- **Generate the handed-in footage too** ([RFC](GENERATED_FOOTAGE_RFC.md)): an opt-in flag on the CLI and the skill that sends each shot to an image or video model you choose (local, your own API key through an aggregator, or your agent's tools): B-roll for motion-graphics stories as well as character shots, made as a still first and animated only once the still passes, laid out from the frame the animatic drew, with the character's description pasted word for word, a price quoted first, a budget, and cheap drafts before finals. Every generated shot goes through the same checks as one you hand in. Handing in your own footage stays the default.
- Curated music and sound packs per style.
- **A library of end cards:** twenty to thirty end-card layouts instead of one (a quiet sign-off on paper, the URL as the hero, the last frame held with type on a scrim, a split panel, a framed and stamped page, and more), each one dressed in the brand's own fonts, colours and logo. The ending also follows what the call to action actually is: a website, a comment keyword ("comment DECK below for the checklist"), follow, save or download each get layouts made for them, not a URL pill with other words in it. And it need not be a separate card: the close can play over the supplied footage, with the action set as type on the moving shot. The style's seed and the inputs pick one (the action, real footage or none, the logo's shape and tone), so two videos rarely end the same way. Any logo works: the mark is measured once at import, a dark mark on a transparent background is recoloured to read, a light one is kept, and a logo with its own solid background sits whole on a plate instead of being tinted into a block. Builds on `brand-lockup`'s `end` variant and the title variants `cta` already has.
- **A subject inventory before the concepts:** before pitching ideas, the planner lists eight to twelve things only this subject has (its place, its numbers, its constraint, the objects in its world), and the winning concept must be built from one of them, so a video about the 20-20-20 rule opens on a real view twenty feet away rather than a generic office.
- **A dated registry of video tells** ([RFC](VIDEO_TELLS_RFC.md)): a list of devices that make a video read as generated (a quote card over a wide set, a dissolve between every scene, a centred title card as the hook, a fact card fighting the caption), each dated and enforced by the finish gates, so a draft that leans on them fails before you see it.
- **The rest of the motion check:** the first rules run before the render ([Direction guardrails](DIRECTION_GUARDRAILS.md), section 8): entrances ease out, exits ease in, nothing slides at one speed, an exit is not slower than its entrance, and a cascade is over within two seconds. Still to come: the hero as the biggest move (the plan has to say which element is the hero and how big each one is), holding a settled scene to a third of its elements moving at once, and a repair pass that fixes a finding by itself.
- **Fuller motion grammars:** each grammar now sets how duration grows with travel distance, how much shorter an exit is than its entrance, how far children trail their parent, parallax by depth, and how much the rest of the frame dims behind the hero ([Motion grammar](KIT_CONTRACTS.md#motion-grammar-kitcoregrammarmjs)). Exits, world-camera parallax and the focus dim follow them everywhere they are shared. Still to do: travel distance and follow-through reach only the world camera and its overlays, not elements inside modules, and modules that dim their own highlights keep their own level. Interface timing numbers are rescaled for video.
- **Exploded-view object scenes:** the idea is shown as one isometric machine of solid parts, in four tones with a single lit accent and no arrows, that comes apart and snaps back. The motion is built frame by frame. A first version has shipped as the `exploded-view` module: three built-in objects (a server, a phone, a battery pack) of 15 to 27 boxes and cylinders, or a parts list, drawn the same by both renderers. Still to come: objects of forty or more parts, more built-in objects, parts lists written by the planner, a portrait layout that fills the frame, and an explode timed to the words that name each part.
- **More from Openverse and the hand-drawn marks:** Openverse audio once research sources sound, a colour option for the light leak, and marks on captions as well as titles.
- **Real 3D scenes with three.js** ([RFC](WEBGL_SCENES_RFC.md)): research into drawing the 3D modules with real lighting, shadows and depth of field, a device that shows your own footage on its screen, and a 3D environment behind the world camera, with both renderers kept in step.
- **Fluid, connected motion, in explainers and animation alike:** a video where every element is built to move and nothing just appears in a box. R&D doc: [FLUID_MOTION_RFC.md](FLUID_MOTION_RFC.md). **Shipped (phase 1):** `node-graph` (nodes flock out of a hub and fan above it, glowing wires with dots streaming down them, pulses that land on a beat grid and light the hub, every wire firing together at `converge` with an impact shake and a ripple, idle breath on every element) and the patterns under it in `kit/core/fluid-motion.mjs` (flock, wave, idle, shake, beat grid), in both renderers with parity fixtures; the motion check reads an element that flashes on every beat as a flash. **Shipped (phase 2):** the motion check warns `static-hold` on a settled scene that stops moving for over 1.5 s, fails `shake-overshoot` on an impact shake that grows, does not settle or outlasts 0.3 s, and fails `loop-seam` on a looping module whose last frame differs from its first; an `-idle` wrapper no longer counts as an element for crowding. **Shipped (phase 5, second half):** group entrances and idle breath on the catalogue H modules that draw sets: `icon-grid` flocks and breathes, `drop-grid` rocks on its base and ripples when full, `social-wall`'s field arrives as one wave and its pulled posts breathe, and `item-list` breathes once in; the bob is capped at 4% of an element's height so small sets keep their lines. **Shipped (phase 4):** anchors (`kit/core/anchors.mjs`): any catalogue module can publish named points from its view tree with the time each lands; `node-graph` publishes `hub`, `node-<id>` and `converge`, and a cast cue aimed at `{scene, anchor}` plays as it lands, so a character looks at each card as it lands and cheers at converge (sample: `examples/story/node-graph-anchors-9x16`). **Shipped (phase 3):** elements carried across scenes (`kit/core/carry.mjs`, `kit/core/flow.mjs`). An identical element (a title, a hub) pins across a cut with no exit or entrance. A moved one flies from its old rect to its new slot (a FLIP, slow → fast → slow, the group leaving as a flock). A graphics plate that comes back after a footage cutaway resumes where it left off. `story.motion.flow` turns this on: the `kinetic` and `steady` feels and calm and premium style packs set it by default, a scene's `carry` map overrides the pairing, and `carry: false` opts a scene out (samples: `examples/story/flow-carry-9x16`, `flow-auto-9x16`). Carry also rides a camera pan or whip-pan, in world-camera stories and cuts stories alike, and a push or whip slide: the element's rect is mapped through the two plates' places in the world and it travels with the camera's own move, so a title holds still on screen while the plates slide past and the cards glide into their new slots (R&D: [rd/carry-moving-camera.md](rd/carry-moving-camera.md); samples: `examples/story/flow-camera-9x16`, `flow-slide-9x16`). Through a camera push, pull, orbit or overview the carried elements ride a layer that cancels the camera, so a title holds still, upright and at its size while the plates zoom or turn under it (§6.6; samples: `examples/story/flow-zoom-9x16`, `flow-orbit-9x16`, `flow-overview-9x16`). **Shipped (phase 5, first half):** planner-drawn graphics (`kit/core/graphic.mjs`): the planner may describe a member as `{graphic: {kind, …}}` from a closed list (a card with its own icon, label and count badge, a chip, a badge, a ribbon, or a world-kit prop; any icon; text that fits), validated like module params and drawn by one painter in the brand's palette and type; `node-graph` nodes and `icon-grid` items take one (sample: `examples/story/graphics-9x16`). **Shipped (growth from existing elements, §6.4):** `sprout` grows `node-graph`'s wires (glow, streaming dots, pulses, the label lit as a pulse lands) from another scene's anchor to new labels, so nothing new appears unattached; it works in one scene and across a cut when the source element is pinned into the next scene, and an unknown anchor or a source that leaves first is a validation error. `concept-card` publishes `card` and `icon`, and anchors now carry their box (sample: `examples/story/sprout-ad-card-9x16`). **Shipped (phase 6):** loops. `scene.loop: true` on a catalogue H or I module makes its last frame its first: every element goes back to its entrance pose under the exit, idle breath and pulses fit the scene, and the motion check's `loop-seam` passes. A short that loops to its opener (`ending.loop_to`) now hands back on its last frame, and the check warns `loop-target` when the loop returns to a scene other than the opener (sample: `examples/story/loop-9x16`). **Next:** the container morph the R&D sample backed (new `width`, `height` and `radius` op props in both renderers, then carry pairs whose shape changes morph the box instead of scaling it) and wire ends that follow a carried (moving) source. Still to do from the list below:
  - Elements carry from one scene into the next and settle into a new layout (a row of cards flies up into a fan above a character) instead of cutting to a fresh layout.
  - Larger groups move together with a shared pattern: a flock, a wave, a fan, a march in unison, each member slightly offset so the group reads as alive rather than mechanical.
  - Every element has its own small idle motion while it is on screen, tied to the motion grammar, so a settled frame still breathes.
  - Node graphs with glowing wires, and pulses of light that travel along them to show data or work moving from one node to the next, with highlights that light a node as a pulse arrives.
  - Characters react to the graphics around them: they look at a card that lands, raise their arms as a fan opens.
  - Graphics drawn for the project (a card with its own icon, a prop, a badge) by the planner, in the style's palette and type, rather than only from the fixed module catalogue.
- **Member modules past the drawn-graphic list:** the `graphic` spec stays a closed list (card, chip, badge, ribbon, world-kit prop), and anything past it becomes its own catalogue module ([FLUID_MOTION_RFC.md](FLUID_MOTION_RFC.md) §10). None of the example stories work around a missing graphic yet. These are the member forms that other modules already take, so a node-graph or icon-grid member will want them next. Each would be a module whose members can join a set:
  - A member with a second line under its label (a "sub"), which about 8 modules already take: map pins, grid-race, cell-grid, title-decompose terms, and the results, card-wall and downloads cards. Built: the `sub-card` member (`{member: {module: "sub-card", label, sub, icon?}}` on node-graph and icon-grid).
  - A member in its own colour or tone, which about 5 modules take (map highlights and pins, the results cards, cell-grid, grid-race). Built (the `tone` member module; in the next release): a card in a step of the brand palette (`accent-deep`, `primary-light`), never a hex, kept at 3:1 on bg.
  - A person as a member, shown as a photo, avatar or initials tile (built; in the next release: `{member: {module: "person", name, image?, shape?}}` on node-graph and icon-grid, an initials tile in the brand palette when there is no photo). Still to come: person-id, lineage-graph, map pin cards, commit authors and the repo-pr-card author drawing their people through it.
  - A value or multiplier longer than three characters ("$7.15", "×3.2"), as on map-highlight and cell-grid. Built: the `value-card` member (`{member: {module: "value-card", value, label?}}` on node-graph and icon-grid).
  - A brand mark instead of an icon (built; in the next release: `{member: {module: "brand-mark", src, label?}}` on node-graph and icon-grid, measured once at import and recoloured, kept or plated like an end-card logo). Still to come: particle-logo taking a measured mark the same way.
  - A member whose status changes over time (pending, pass, fail), which status-rack and the results cards do for the whole module. Built (the `status` member module; in the next release): a card that goes pending → active → pass or fail at times or on beats, colour and corner mark crossing over slow → fast → slow.
  - One-off, not yet a candidate: a progress bar inside a card (only the custom-module test fixture has one; progress-ring and percent-fill are nearby).
- **List items as separate elements, never a boxed list** (built; in the next release): a list (steps, features, a checklist) is drawn as one element per item, each with its own entrance (`item-list`): its own pill chip, or free type stacked, fanned, scattered or orbiting a subject, with ticks landing one at a time. The finish gate's `boxed-list` warning flags a checklist card of checkbox rows as a tell.
- **A fixed imperfection in every character** (built; in the next release): one small, deliberate flaw kept in `must_keep`, so drift is easy to spot for the vision check and for a person. It is a rig param (`flaw`: a plaster on one cheek, a scar through one eyebrow, scratches on one cheek), drawn on the face in every drawing style for people and kinds alike; `bible freeze` and `cast create` give one to a character that has none, and the critic is told it beside each sheet. Each bible character, and each character you design with `cast create`, also gets a word-for-word description from its rig params: silhouette, proportions, colours with hex codes, one signature item, one gesture, `must_keep` and the flaw. Still to come: flaws on props or clothing (a chipped mug, a dented antenna, a scuffed shoe), and the full turnaround reference sheet (front, both profiles, back, three-quarter).
- **The breakdown reel** (built; in the next release): `launchclip reel <project>` turns a finished project into a 10 s "how it was made" loop on a 120 BPM grid. A hook lands word by word on the beat, the finished shot plays, then the frame tilts into an isometric stack whose layers peel apart one per beat with labels. The layers are the project's own artefacts: the bible's reference sheet, the animatic frame and the final for an animation; the storyboard, the style, the hero scene drawn plain and the final for an explainer. They snap back on a beat with a short shake, and a payoff line settles on the opening frame, so the last frame is the first (`launchclip reel check` measures the seam). Built on the `layer-stack` module ([contracts](KIT_CONTRACTS.md)), with the exploded-view motion.
- **A before-and-after wipe** (built; in the next release): a handle drags across one shot, and the other version (a filter, the redesign, the raw take) is revealed behind it, slow → fast → slow with no overshoot. As a transition (`before-after`: the next scene opens behind the handle, optionally resting half-way, a label on each side) and as a media layout in one scene (`before-after`: two sources, or one with a grade on one side, the handle resting and reversing at its stops). Both renderers draw the same frames ([contract](KIT_CONTRACTS.md)).
- **A series bible with an engine** (partly built; in the next release): a show bible records the engine, the thing that goes wrong every episode and can never be solved for good, with the episodes pitched from it. The writers' room checks the premise before episode one (`make --animation --engine "…"`): if it cannot pitch five distinct episode ideas from the engine, it says the engine is too weak, proposes a stronger one and writes nothing; a bible whose engine gives fewer than five is refused. Still to come: series bibles in collections, so every episode of a series shares the one bible and its engine ([Animation RFC](ANIMATION_PIPELINE_RFC.md), phase 13).
- **Explainers that show the claim, not just say it** ([plan](EXPLAINER_CLAIMS_RFC.md)): general rules for any business's footage-led or mixed explainer (a trade comparing materials, a clinic explaining a treatment, a SaaS comparing plans), learned from reviewing a real comparison short whose footage was strong but whose middle went flat. Each rule is planned, then checked by a gate.
  - **One concrete fact per point:** in a list or comparison, every point carries one specific number, object or consequence ("chair legs dent it", "hot underfoot at midday", "$X over ten years"), not a category word ("cheapest", "low maintenance"). The script check flags a point with nothing a viewer could picture, and the visuals take their subject from that fact.
  - **A hook that proves its claim:** the opening shows the loss or the payoff (the greyed board beside the new one, the bill, the before and after) inside its first seconds, rather than stating it over a wide shot. Builds on the measured hook in [Sharper plans and a faster hook](PLAN_AND_PACE_RFC.md).
  - **The payoff is the richest moment:** the beat the video exists for (the cost comparison, the answer, the result) is planned as the densest and most moving scene, drawn as a chart, a counter or a side-by-side, never as two words on a wash; pacing and motion rise into it, and the density and motion gates measure it against the rest.
  - **Annotate the footage itself:** a circle, an arrow, a measured line or a label pinned to the object in the shot (the dent, the faded boards, the button), drawn over the footage and held on it through the camera's move, so supplied footage carries the point instead of a card beside it. Works on any footage or screenshot a customer hands in.
  - **An illustration of the line when no shot fits:** the fallback for a line with no matching footage is an editorial illustration of that line's object or consequence (a sun arc greying a plank, a thermometer on a deck, a brush and a calendar of weekends) on a full-bleed plate or a material close-up, in the style's palette. A flat panel, a small boxed photo or a gradient-only plate stops being a fallback; the finish gate's flat-surface share already measures the result.
  - **One idea per frame:** besides the caption, a frame carries one block of on-screen text (a label, a title or a short list), not a title, a sub-line, bullets and a caption at once. A gate counts the text blocks on screen at each moment and flags crowding.
  - **Footage stays the hero under type:** a scrim may darken or tint a shot behind text, never bleach it to a pale wash; a gate flags footage whose contrast under an overlay drops too far, and type on footage takes a local scrim or a position on a calm area instead.
  - **Repairs that apply the motion findings:** when the motion gates name a still span and the move that fixes it (a drift through a hold, a camera kick on a stressed word), the repair pass makes that change and re-checks, instead of holding the render for review. Builds on the motion check's planned repair pass.
- **Upload details from the script:** after a render, write a draft title, description, tags and YouTube chapter timestamps. The chapter times come from the real word timings of each chapter, so they match the video, and the description is written from what the script says. A draft for you to check, never uploaded for you.
- **Shorts from a long video:** find the parts of a long render or recording that make sense on their own, add the one line of setup each needs so it works without the rest of the video, reframe it to 9:16 and caption it. Launch kits cut down a launch video; this finds the best stand-alone moments in a long one. Its edges can use calm-cut scoring (`calmCut` in `kit/core/calm-cut.mjs`, already used by `launchclip cutdown`): the stillest moment in the silence around the first and last word, a cut of the long form first.
- **Fast scoring for research, hooks and scripts** ([roadmap](SCORING_ROADMAP.md)): a new kind of model answers closed questions about content in a fraction of a second. Use it to check every hook and script against a brand's own criteria, tag a library of videos from your niche to see which hooks and angles are crowded or open, write thousands of options and keep a varied shortlist, and learn from real results what works for one brand. Local by default, hosted models with your own key.
- **Stick figures that act, not just pose** (built; in the next release; test stories in `examples/story/stickman-box-9x16` and `stickman-void-9x16`): the stick cast gets physical comedy built on cause and effect. Also built alongside: a classic black-and-white `void-stage`, two filled worlds (`parlour`, `cafe-terrace`) and `depth: true` light and depth on any world. Still open: the density gate ignores the camera, a landing on a contact action should frame the target too, and the painted texture is faint at video size.
  - **A gag is a chain:** wind-up → contact → the force passes into the thing hit → an exaggerated result → a rebound and a reaction. A paper stack squashes when slapped, its sheets lag and spring back, and the actor flinches. Today's actions only look, point, walk, stand and stretch (`kit/core/characters/actions.mjs`); this adds push, pull, hit, catch, lift and drop.
  - **Hands that hold things:** a hand targets a grip point on a prop, reach is capped so arms never stretch, and a handoff runs giver alone → both holding → receiver alone, with the prop owned by one hand at a time. Today the only held prop is a phone.
  - **A gag squash of its own:** a whole-body flatten or stretch is a separate timed layer with a planned start, size and recovery, so normal acting keeps fixed bone lengths. The small torso dip in `MOVE.squash` stays for ordinary moves.
  - **A rubber-hose drawing style:** thin single-stroke limbs that bend in curves rather than at a hard elbow, a white round face with two hair tufts, small hands and curved feet, in finely inked early-cartoon rooms in muted sage, grey and ivory with tiny red accents. A new drawing style beside `stick`, with a matching world palette.
  - **Ink that varies in weight:** a pen whose stroke thickens and thins along the line (`pens.mjs` already has `jitter`), and a check that limb weight stays the same at a matched head size, so figures don't drift into thin wire.
  - **A camera that lands on the subject:** a move names its subject and where it ends up on screen, slides sideways for a reveal, pushes in for a reaction, and cuts between places. Builds on the camera-motion work.
  - **Motion reviewed against a reference clip:** per shot, did the promised move happen, does the gesture read, does the reaction build, and do limbs keep their weight. A technical pass is not a visual pass. An animation section in `eval/`.
- **Better generation briefs for drawn characters** (built except one clip for several shots, which is designed in the RFC; [RFC](GENERATED_FOOTAGE_RFC.md)): story-shot briefs (`src/generate/brief.js`) that hold a drawn style the way they already hold a character.
  - **A locked drawing paragraph per style:** each drawing style gets a fixed paragraph for its line, palette and light, pasted word for word into every keyframe and plate brief, in place of "drawn in the stick style".
  - **Anatomy negatives per style:** for stick figures, exactly two arms and two legs, a raised arm replaces the lowered one, limbs stay connected, the spine stays one thin line, no filled torso. Added to `STORY_NEGATIVE` for that style only.
  - **Action as cause and effect:** timed beats that say setup, action, consequence and reaction, naming each grip, release, foot contact and change in a prop, in place of "Nothing else moves". Every beat changes the situation; the last earns its hold.
  - **An audio line:** tell the video model "no music, no speech" when the mix is ours, and say whether to keep its own sound effects.
  - **One clip, several shots** (needs an RFC section first): plan the edit, then let one 10–15 s generation carry several motivated shots, with a ledger of which part of each clip is kept, instead of one generation per shot.
  - **Continuity between clips** (same RFC section): for continuous action, the last frame kept in the edit becomes the next clip's first frame, and the brief names the pose, grip, prop and direction carried over. For providers that take reference inputs, an option to pass the style as a reference instead of a first frame; the checked keyframe stays the default.
- **A hand-drawn reveal look:** an illustration scene drawn in the way a person would draw it, not wiped on in one sweep. Today the `pen-plotter` look plots a frame row by row and the hand-drawn marks only reach titles; this draws a generated or supplied illustration plate, in both renderers.
  - **Object by object:** the plate is split into its parts (a figure's head and body, each prop, the background), and each part is finished before the next starts, the subject first.
  - **Outline, then the inside:** within a part the outer contour goes down first, and inner lines grow out of lines already on the page, never starting in empty space. Dark fills are not hinted in as noise ahead of their lines.
  - **Sketch, ink, colour:** an optional light under-sketch that is rubbed out once the ink is down, ink whose weight follows pen pressure, then marker colour from light to dark in short strokes near the last one, finishing as a flat fill that matches the plate.
  - **A stroke budget from the narration:** the seconds a line of narration gives a plate set how many strokes it can have (at a steady strokes-per-second rate per drawing method), and the plate brief is written to that budget, so a busy picture is never squeezed into a short line by speeding the pen up.
  - **A hand and pen, opt-in:** a drawn hand holding the pen follows the stroke, in a few pens (marker, pencil, brush, fountain pen) or as the pen alone, with optional brand or sponsor text on the barrel that is in every frame.
  - **Words written on:** key words named in the narration are written by hand as they are said, in the order of their strokes, reusing the hand-drawn title marks.
